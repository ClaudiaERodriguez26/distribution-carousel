Segments
  • Focus on individuals
  • Focus on semi-urban and rural areas
Consequence: Product Strategy
  • Individuals want many colors (large product variety)
  • They want different package sizes
Consequence: Distribution Strategy
  • Disperses segment needs across a large distribution network
  • Different colors and sizes means a large product range
  • Low-income customers require low prices; these consumers buy only a few cans of paint on each shopping occasion
  • Seasonal demand means excess inventory must be carried in distribution channels during non-peak months to even production
  • Requires dealers who can deal in small amounts (not in bulk)
  • Small dealers mean intermediate storage in order to pick up frequent shipments
  • All indicates a very costly distribution system—but recall low consumer income necessitates low prices
Solution: Channel Design
  • Product assortment over 2000 Stock Keeping Units (SKUs)
  • Network of factories (4), depots (3), and dealers (15,000)
  • Inventory management (28 days; industry average is 45 days)
  • Credit control – dealer incentives 5% cash discount; 3.5% special discount
  • Use satellite communications and Intranet
  • Track online sales data to capture fast-moving items
  • Save money in distribution to spend on promotion (10% of sales)
