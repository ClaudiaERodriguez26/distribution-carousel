Customer Segmentation: Customers are segmented into categories based on their worth to the company. This can be determined by current sales volume, profitability, potential for growth, strategic importance, or a combination of these and other factors.

Value Assessment: The value of a customer may include their direct monetary value (how much revenue they bring in), their lifetime value (the net profit attributed to the entire future relationship with the customer), and less tangible factors like referral potential or strategic position in the market.

Resource Allocation: Sales efforts are then allocated according to these segments. High-value customers receive more attention; e.g., more frequent visits, faster service, or access to better deals and offers. This ensures that the sales force's time and the company's resources are invested where they're likely to yield the highest return.

Tailored Sales Strategies: Different strategies may be developed for different customer segments. For high-worth customers, a company might employ a more consultative selling approach, while for lower-worth customers, the approach might be more transactional.

Efficiency and Effectiveness: Focusing sales efforts on customer worth ensures that sales activities are both efficient (minimal waste of resources) and effective (maximizing sales results).

Performance Monitoring: The sales force's performance is monitored to ensure that the effort is indeed correlating with the expected return on investment from each customer segment.

Dynamic Adjustment: Over time, customers' worth may change, so sales strategies and efforts need to be adjusted accordingly. A once high-worth customer may reduce their order volume, or a low-worth customer might show potential for growth.
